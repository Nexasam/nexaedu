# The Alert - Creative Brief

## Core Idea

A financially pressured Christian receives a large accidental bank transfer. His closest friend calls it an answer to prayer. He must decide whether relief that requires dishonesty can truly be called provision.

## Purpose

Create a contemporary Christian drama that treats integrity as costly discipleship rather than a shortcut to an immediate material reward.

## Audience

- Nigerian Christians aged 18-45
- Young adults navigating work, money, faith, and social pressure
- Online audiences watching YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, and church media channels

## Format

- Working title: **The Alert**
- Runtime: 3 minutes
- Aspect ratio: 16:9 master, protected for a later 9:16 crop
- Genre: Contemporary Christian moral drama
- Tone: Intimate, tense, grounded, restrained
- Location: One modest Lagos apartment
- Speaking characters: Two
- Time: Early evening

## Message

Not every unexpected opportunity is God's provision. A blessing does not require disobedience, concealment, or a damaged conscience.

## Scripture Anchor

Proverbs 10:9

Use the scripture reference on the final card. Select and license a preferred Bible translation before quoting the verse verbatim in a published version.

## Creative Guardrails

- Do not reward Daniel immediately after he returns the money.
- Do not portray Femi as evil or foolish; his reasoning should feel tempting and human.
- Avoid denominational language and church stereotypes.
- Avoid excessive preaching. Let the decision carry the message.
- Keep banking interfaces generic and free of real bank branding.
- Do not show account numbers, personal information, or realistic transaction credentials.
- Keep performances understated and recognisably Nigerian.

## Visual Direction

- Cinematic naturalism
- Muted warm-brown apartment tones contrasted with cool blue phone light
- Realistic Lagos middle-class details without visual clutter
- Mostly locked or gently moving camera
- Shallow depth of field during the moral confrontation
- No glossy prosperity imagery

## Success Criteria

The audience should:

1. Understand the financial pressure within the first 30 seconds.
2. Feel that keeping the money is genuinely tempting.
3. Believe both characters' points of view.
4. Understand why Daniel reports the transfer through the bank instead of sending money directly to a stranger.
5. Leave discussing whether they would make the same decision.
